# Isometric projection (island view)

**Status:** draft — rules derived from sprite measurements, visual checks and static analysis of the original ([`docs/re/annodisplay.md`](../re/annodisplay.md), [`docs/re/annowelt.md`](../re/annowelt.md)); not yet compared with screenshots of the original. Open points in OQ-026.

The original renders differently in structure: ground is a mesh of Gouraud-shaded, depth-buffered textured triangles, and sprites are depth-tested per pixel (`z` bytes are depth offsets). This renderer is a painter's-algorithm approximation that uses the same projection constants.

Implementation: `anno_render::iso` (CPU reference renderer), `anno_render::scene` (scenario composition), `anno-extract view`, `anno1503 --view`.

## Elevation
- `HEIGHT[x, y]` is the height of tile corner `(x, y)`; tile `(x, y)` spans corners `(x, y)`, `(x+1, y)`, `(x, y+1)`, `(x+1, y+1)` (D: `AnnoWelt.dll` corner grid with stride width + 1, `0x100900c0`, `0x10084870`; the corner reading of `HEIGHT[x, y]` is likely). Each tile is two triangles split along the fixed diagonal from corner `(x, y+1)` to `(x+1, y)` (D: `AnnoWelt.dll` `0x1009b920`, indices A,B,C and C,B,D); the renderer interpolates height inside each triangle. The original also shades each corner from the terrain normal (D: `0x100a03c0`, light vector not traced) — not reproduced.
- One height unit = 0.04 world units (D: `AnnoWelt.dll` table `0x100E35E0`); one world unit up = 51.477 px at zoom group 0 (D: `AnnoFrame.dll` camera `0x10071d70`: scale 59.1633, tilt 29.53°). `iso::HEIGHT_LIFT` = 2.059 px per unit at group 0 (scaled by tile width for groups 1, 2). The same camera gives 83.67 × 41.24 px per tile at zoom 0, consistent with the 84 × 42 sprites.
- Flat raised tiles (tried first) produce terraces and gaps; per-corner slopes remove them (visual).
- Rasterisation (implementation): each triangle is filled in screen space, every pixel centre inside it once (small edge tolerance so shared edges leave no cracks), with the texture position interpolated from the corners (a planar triangle projects affinely, so this is exact). Replaced an oversampled forward mapping on 2026-10-17: a 1920 × 1080 view went from 477 ms to 25 ms.
- Open water (texture 0) and textures 1–10 are not part of the original's terrain mesh (D: `AnnoWelt.dll` `0x1009b920`).

## Animation and layers
- A sprite set's `ANIM` record gives the animation: bytes 2–3 frames per animation, bytes 4–5 milliseconds per frame (bit 25 of the `u32` at 4 is a flag of unknown meaning). Frames per set = animation frames × 1 or × 4 rotations (P, all sets). Frame shown = `rotation · frames + (time / ms + phase) mod frames`; phase = the item's phase bits (surf: `(3y + 5x) mod 32`), `(5x + 3y) mod 32` for the sea. The original divides its tick by `ms / 2` (D: `0x100a03c0`); the tick unit is not traced, so the renderer uses the `ANIM` ms per frame (possibly half the original speed).
- Only the water layer (sea, rivers, waterfalls, surf) is animated so far; other animated objects (e.g. mills, fires) show their first frame.
- Layers (`scene::Layers`), in order: ground; water objects below sea level; the sea surface (whole region); water objects at or above sea level; other objects. Ground and objects are static per zoom group.
- Frame pipeline (`anno_render::frame`, used by the viewer and the benchmark): static data (ground, objects, the per-pixel sea field `sea::WaterField`) is built per 512 × 512 px chunk by background workers and cached. The workers take jobs from a priority list that every frame rewrites (visible chunks, then a ring of one chunk around the view, then the same area in the neighbouring zoom groups); a chunk not built yet shows open sea. A frame then composes ground → water objects below sea level (at the frame time) → sea (per-pixel lookup into `sea::SeaPattern`, the periodic sea image of each animation step, rendered once per zoom group) → water objects above sea level → objects.
- Two compositors with the same rules: the GPU (`anno_render::gpu`, wgpu, shader `gpu.wgsl`; chunks as textures, sea pattern as texture arrays, water objects from a sprite atlas, two render passes) and the CPU (`FrameRenderer::render`, row bands on all cores). Integer pixel positions and `textureLoad` keep the GPU exact: identical to the CPU within 1 per channel for the original water (`bench_frame --check`). Measurements: [`docs/perf/README.md`](../perf/README.md).
- The viewer uses the GPU when available (`--renderer auto|gpu|cpu`), presents with vsync and shows the renderer and frames per second in the title; while the window is occluded it retries every 100 ms instead of spinning. The CPU fallback redraws at the display refresh rate. `Original` water changes image once per 50 ms animation step, as the sprite's `ANIM` record; `Enhanced` cross-fades between consecutive steps.
- Shadows are 37.5 % black drawn over the destination (= 5/8 brightness on opaque pixels), so they also work on a transparent object layer.

## Reference: gameplay video of the original (2026-10-17)
Source: YouTube video `2I-T_8SAFuU` (Anno 1503 History Edition — the 2020 re-release of the same engine and data; differences from GOG 2.0.0.5 are possible), 7:50–12:00 at 1920 × 1080, analysed locally; no frames are kept in the repository.
- **No breaking waves.** Coasts go from sand to translucent shallow water (sea floor visible, lighter near the shore) to deep blue; no foam lines on beaches, only a ship's wake. Confirms that the faithful water has no breakers and that `WaterStyle::Enhanced` is an addition.
- **Water animates:** in a static camera shot, water pixels change ≈ 4 × as much as land pixels between frames 0.2 s apart (land change = compression noise).
- **Loop period ≈ 0.8 s:** frame-to-frame difference of a water patch is smallest at 24 video frames (0.8 s at 30 fps) and largest around 12–16 frames. That matches 16 frames × 50 ms from the sea sprite's `ANIM` record, i.e. one animation step per `ANIM` ms, and rules out the double speed (0.4 s) that `tick / (ms / 2)` with a millisecond tick would give (OQ-026).
- **Colour:** open sea averages RGB (28, 89, 138) in the video, ours (37, 107, 142) at zoom 0 — the original is darker and less green. Candidate cause: the colour parameter `0xC0` passed with every water sprite (selects a colour-table variant, not applied yet; `docs/re/annodisplay.md`). The original's water texture also looks finer and more sparkling.

## Enhanced water (not in the original, opt-in)
Off by default; `E` in the viewer, `--water enhanced` for `--png`. Applies to sea pixels only, so cached ground and objects are unaffected. Not derived from the original (which has no breakers, see "Reference"); the owner's target is crashing waves on coasts and open sea (PLAN §0.2). Tuning lives in `iso::Waves::DEFAULT` (to become configuration); `gpu.wgsl` mirrors it.

Reworked 2026-10-17 after the colleague's review recording, which showed white blobs forming a dash pattern at zoom group 2 ("Rasterstreifen"), thin lines parallel to the coast, angular coastal bands and a muddy shallow band. Current model (`iso::wave_shade` → light, foam, tint):
- Open water: slow swell from two drifting layers of smooth value noise (brightness ± 7 %); no whitecaps.
- Waves on every side (revised again 2026-10-17, owner: "waves are only rendered along one axis"): two wave systems blended by **amplitude**, the common approach for shoreline waves: an open-sea swell from one direction, and near the coast (within ≈ 3 tiles, `ws` = smoothstep over the smoothed shore distance) a surf system whose crests are lines of equal shore distance (`dist / 1.8 tiles + t / T`), so they run parallel to the shore and travel towards it on every side of an island. Blending the two phases instead compresses crests into bunches of thin lines; blending their crest profiles does not. Breaking, foam and swash use the blended crest; swash follows the surf system.
- Rivers (enhanced, 2026-10-17 after owner feedback "edges way too sharp, unnatural"): the depth-tested sprites keep the hard, triangle-shaped cut of the terrain and the overlapping 3 × 3 pieces show as patches. Enhanced water therefore draws river pieces (`scene::RIVER_ANLAGEN` 2701, 2705–2707) itself, like the sea coast: a river surface level per height corner (item height + 9, `sea::RIVER_LEVEL`; averaged where pieces overlap, smoothed twice and spread one corner), water where the river bed lies below it, with a soft edge (bed = `sea::river_bed_height`, an interpolating Catmull-Rom surface, not `smooth_height`, and never narrower than the painted bed of the river sprites: "River gaps" below); the bed shows through (openness ≈ 0.4, `sea::RIVER_OPENNESS`); a slow drifting shimmer, no waves (`iso::river_shade`). Waterfalls and river mouths (2702–2704): see "Rivers into the sea" below. Water-field flag bit 1 marks river pixels.
- Wave events and run-up physics (2026-10-17, owner: water line too uniform, no visible extra movement, "more randomness, physics behind the waves"): each surf crest is an event with its own relative height (`iso::wave_height`: sets of large waves via a slow envelope over the wave number, random per wave, varying smoothly along the coast); the surf clock drifts so periods are irregular (`iso::surf_time`, monotonic). Waves break where the depth falls below H / γ (breaker index γ = 0.78), so large waves break further out. Run-up follows Hunt's formula R = ξ·H with the Iribarren number ξ = tan β / √(H / L) (`iso::runup_height`; H = 6 `HEIGHT` units for the largest waves, gain 1.6, horizontal reach ≤ 2 tiles), using the beach slope per pixel (smooth terrain gradient, water-field flag bits 2–7). Rush-up time grows with wave height, the backwash is slower (gravity). Foam appears on impact, scaled by the wave's height. The earlier run-up was set in `HEIGHT` units only, which on steeper beaches moved the waterline by a fraction of a tile and was not visible. This is a parametric physical model per wave, not a fluid simulation.
- `gpu::tests::shader_is_valid_wgsl` validates the shader with naga, so WGSL errors fail the tests instead of the viewer.
- Coastal waves (revised 2026-10-17 after owner feedback "flat, like a light reflection, lines tracing the shoals"; causes: crests followed the shore distance and so traced every lobe and shoal, waves had no height profile, breaking ignored depth): swell from one direction (`Waves::swell_dir`, diagonal on screen), crest phase `along / 2.6 tiles + t / 3400 ms + bend` with two noise scales bending the crests. Each crest has a height profile (gentle back, rounded top, steep front) shaded as relief (front darker, top brighter). Waves grow from 12 to 4 `HEIGHT` units of depth, break where the depth falls below ≈ 4 (narrow foam line on the crest, short whitewater trail, broken into pieces by noise) and run on as weaker bores over the flats; swash runs up the beach below 1.5 units. Shallow water is tinted by depth (8 units). Mixing the shore distance into the phase (to turn crests towards each beach) was tried and dropped: it compressed the phase into bunches of thin lines at shoal edges.
- Waterline: the original's waterline follows its terrain triangles, so it is straight between tile corners and steps along tile edges where the height changes within a tile (owner: "at water level you never find straight lines", except at buildings). Enhanced water decides where water is from a smooth surface instead: a cubic B-spline through the corner heights (no overshoot) plus fixed value noise of ±2 `HEIGHT` units (`sea::smooth_height`, `Waves::shore_noise`), with a soft edge of ±0.35 units (`Waves::shore_edge`). Water may thus cover the lowest beach pixels or leave the shallowest floor dry (relative to the waterline at 16); buildings and other objects are drawn on top as before. The signed depth over that surface is stored per pixel (`WaterField::depth`, 1/4 units around 128) next to a flag for the original's waterline (`WaterField::flags`); the original mode uses only the flag.
- Deep water hides the sea floor: the floor fades into open water from 4 to 14 `HEIGHT` units below the waterline (`iso::deep_water`, over the smooth surface of the waterline). The terrain height is smooth across tiles, so this removes the tile-shaped edges between the original's floor textures (shallows, sand, deep sea) that showed as a stepped boundary between near-island water and deep sea (owner feedback 2026-10-17). The original mode keeps the original floor.
- Shallow water within 2.2 tiles of the shore is tinted towards turquoise (RGB 0.36, 0.74, 0.76, up to 30 %).
- Detail level: fine foam texture fades out with the zoom (`iso::wave_detail`, 1 at group 0, 0 at group 2), so nothing aliases into patterns.
- Shore distance: per island, a chamfer distance field (in tiles) from every height-grid corner at or above sea level, smoothed by two 3 × 3 box-blur passes (removes the octagonal kinks), interpolated per pixel and stored in 1/32 tiles (`sea::SHORE_SCALE`; 7.97 tiles = open sea).
- The original's surf sprites (`ANLAGEN` 2708) are not drawn in enhanced mode; its own surf replaces them (they showed as dark diamonds over the tinted shallows).
- Smooth animation: the sea cross-fades between consecutive sprite steps (the original jumps every 50 ms).
- Smooth surface: each sea pixel averages the 2 × 2 pattern pixels at its position (premultiplied alpha), so the sprite's 50 % checkerboard (`BSH_RGBWZ`), which showed as fine diagonal hatching on high-resolution displays, becomes a continuous surface; the floor still shows through at 50 %.
- Deterministic: a pure function of position and time (value noise from an integer hash), so `--png --time` is reproducible.
- Evaluation: the GPU evaluates it per pixel; the CPU path on a grid of 4 / 2 / 2 px (groups 0 / 1 / 2, `sea::SHADING_STEP`), interpolated per pixel, so foam edges differ slightly between the two.
- Not yet: spray above the water at impact, wave interaction with rocks and ships; shore distance across islands (each island's field ends at its rectangle).
- Tests: `iso::tests::wave_noise_is_deterministic_and_bounded`, `crates/app/tests/view.rs::view_png_enhanced_water_is_opt_in`; visual comparison with `bench_frame --check` dumps (`BENCH_DUMP`).

## Wave simulation (enhanced water, GPU)
Owner decision 2026-10-17: a real fluid simulation instead of parametric waves ("still looks too uniform"), built for bigger maps and robust multiplayer. Code: `crates/render/src/fluid.rs` (parameters, swell, window, CPU reference solver), `fluid.wgsl` (compute shader, same arithmetic), `gpu_fluid.rs` (host), sampled in `gpu.wgsl` (`fluid_sample`, `fluid_shade`).

- **Render-only.** Reads terrain, never game state; nothing reads it back. It is not part of the deterministic simulation and cannot affect lockstep multiplayer; frame rate and timing may differ per machine.
- **Scales with the map.** A camera-local window sized to the view (`FluidConfig::window_size`: view span × cells per tile + recentre and sponge margins, at most 2048²; 16 / 8 / 4 cells per tile at zoom groups 0 / 1 / 2, raised from 8 / 4 / 2 on 2026-10-17 for finer waves and shorelines; ≈ 1700² cells at 3840 × 2160, zoom 0), stored as a ring buffer (texel = world cell mod size); it recentres when the view moves 64 cells, and only the newly visible strips get depth and initial state (computed on all CPU cores). Cost is independent of map size and island count; the island lookup for depth is linear in the islands (`Scene::fluid_depth`; replace by a spatial index for very many islands).
- **Set up in the background (2026-10-19).** A window for a new zoom group or size (95–145 ms of CPU work: depth of about 10⁶ cells, initial swell state, 20 MB of texels) is no longer built inside the frame in interactive use (`GpuLimits::background_fluid`, set by `GpuLimits::interactive()`, which the viewer uses; the default and all tests keep the blocking mode). `FluidGpu::update` then starts a job thread that computes the cells straight into the texture byte layout, and uploads the result in slices of at most 2 ms / 16 MiB per frame (`upload_budget_ms`, `upload_bytes_per_frame`) into a second grid (reusing the textures of the window replaced when the size is the same); a recentre does the same for its strips. The window in use keeps simulating until the new one is complete, the shader uses the analytic waves where no window covers a pixel (`fluid_inside`), and a request for another group or size cancels the job in flight. The new window starts from the initial state at the time the job started (the simulation then catches up, at most 12 steps per frame). Result: no frame of the viewer waits for the window (`docs/perf/README.md`, 2026-10-19). Visible consequence, to be decided by the owner: for 0.1–0.4 s after a zoom change the coast shows the analytic enhanced waves instead of the simulation.
- **Model.** Shallow-water equations on a staggered grid (elevation η in `HEIGHT` units, velocity in tiles/s), symplectic Euler, `dt` = 1/120 s (stable for dx = 1/16 tile at 3 tiles/s), up to 12 steps per frame (skips ahead when behind). Wave speed √(g·D): shoaling, refraction, diffraction and reflection emerge. Gravity follows from 3 tiles/s at 16 units depth (≈ real gravity at the game's scale of 1 `HEIGHT` unit = 0.04 world units, 1 tile = 1 world unit; with the first, slower choice of 1.5 tiles/s swash flow was too slow to reach up the beach).
- **Wetting and drying.** Flux with the upwind total depth `η + h` (≥ 0); a face flows only where the higher surface lies above the higher bed; the surface never drops below the bed; swash water above the waterline seeps away (0.4 units/s) so films fall dry. The run-up is simulated: on screen a pixel is water where the surface interpolated from the wet cells lies above the pixel's own smooth terrain.
- **Breaking.** Where the still depth exceeds 3 units, elevation above 0.8 · h is dissipated (rate 8/s) into foam; shallower, the broken wave runs on as a bore and only friction slows it (clipping there stopped all run-up). Swash flow churns foam; foam drifts with the flow (semi-Lagrangian advection from the cell-centre velocity) and decays with τ = 1.6 s; on screen its texture churns (two noise scales evolving over time). Before advection the foam stood where waves keep breaking and read as a static white line (owner feedback 2026-10-17).
- **Incoming swell.** Deep water (depth 10 → 15 units) and a 32-cell sponge at the window border relax to a sum of 8 plane waves with seeded random directions around the full circle (main direction strongest), periods 2.6–4.6 s and amplitudes up to 1.2 units; their interference gives wave groups and an irregular sea, and every coast receives waves.
- **Measured** (CPU reference, synthetic plane beaches, 15 s after warm-up): local shoreline excursion range 0.6–1.1 tiles for beach slopes 0.7–3 units per tile; with the first parameters (slow waves, breaking down to 0.5 units, amplitude 0.35) it was 0.1–0.3 tiles, which the owner saw as "the water does not move".
- **Shading.** Relief lit from the screen's top left (subtle on the open sea, stronger in the surf), foam from the simulation, foam edge at the moving waterline, depth tint; small-scale noise for detail. Rivers keep `river_shade`; the CPU fallback (no GPU) and `--png` keep the parametric model (`iso::wave_shade`).
- **Tests.** `fluid::tests` (window strips, seeded swell, stability, breaking, run-up and backwash on a beach), `gpu_fluid::tests::gpu_matches_cpu_reference` (GPU vs CPU after 40 frames incl. a recentre, < 0.01; skipped without a GPU), WGSL validation of both shaders with naga.
- **C++ port** (2026-10-18, ADR 0001): `src/render/{include/anno/render/fluid.hpp,src/fluid.cpp}` (`anno::render::fluid`), same operation order as `fluid.rs`. `src/render/tests/fluid_test.cpp` repeats `fluid::tests` and the run of `cargo run --release -p anno-render --example digest_fluid` (synthetic beach and island, 300 steps, a ring-buffer recentre and fill, 300 more steps): six FNV-1a digests over the f32 bits equal the Rust ones bit for bit on the same C library — Apple macOS and glibc 2.39 (aarch64 and x86_64) have one table each, selected by the forcing digest, since `sin`/`cos`/`exp` round differently per libm (the two differ by ≤ 1.2·10⁻⁷ over the final state). Elsewhere the swell and five cells are compared within 10⁻⁵ / 10⁻⁴. Deviation: `FluidConfig::window_size` returns `min(max(n, 64), size)` where Rust's `clamp` panics for `size < 64`. Not covered by the parity run (review 2026-10-18): `window_size`/`with_size`, recentres to the left or up, groups other than 0, the unforced interior (a 64-cell window is all sponge), non-power-of-two cell sizes.
- **Cost.** ≈ 1 ms per frame at 3840 × 2160 on the dev machine (`docs/perf/README.md`).

## Rivers into the sea (enhanced water; not in the original)
Tester feedback 2026-10-18: "Der Übergang von Meer zu Flüssen sieht abgehakt und unnatürlich aus" (the transition from sea to rivers looks choppy and unnatural). With enhanced water the waterfall and mouth sprites (FLUESSE 2702–2704; dark-blue patches with tile-straight edges) sat between the smooth river surface and the wave-shaded sea, the river surface ended at the coast about 9 HEIGHT units above the sea, and river and sea shading switched at a hard line. Now (`Scene::water_field_from`, CPU and GPU alike):
- **Shapes from the original:** the waterfall and mouth sprites are not drawn; their alpha (falls with the original's depth test) becomes river water in the water field, blurred to soft edges (≈ 4 px at zoom group 0) — the artists' meandering shapes, the enhanced look. The terrain cannot give these shapes: under falls and mouths the bed is only painted, not carved into `HEIGHT` (tried and discarded 2026-10-18).
- **Falls:** the river surface gets a steep slope there (`kFallRapids`), which `river_shade` turns into a faster, stronger shimmer; **no foam on rivers** (owner, 2026-10-18: foam only at the sea).
- **Into the sea:** river pixels within `kRiverBlend` (16 px at zoom 0) of sea water take on the sea's cover and shading (GPU: the wave simulation's shading sampled there; CPU: `wave_shade` at `kRiverSeaDist`); shallow sea pixels in a gap of at most `kRiverSeam` (4 px) between river and sea water join the river surface, so no sand line remains between them. The original's tile-shaped sea pixels keep bit 0 and their cover, so original water is unchanged.
All constants are tuning values (`scene.hpp`, `iso.hpp`), not evidence. Original water still draws the sprites (digests unchanged); the enhanced scene digests of scenarios with rivers now come from the C++ renderer (`render_corpus_test.cpp`).

## River gaps (enhanced water; fixed 2026-10-19)
Owner report 2026-10-19 (own testing on the Windows PC, enhanced water, a jungle island): stretches of the painted riverbed showed brown bed and no water; in the original every river is connected end to end, because Anno 1503 has no partly dry riverbeds. A `fix`, not a taste question. The screenshot is `Islands/Dschungel/D_256x256_0045_5B.scp` at zoom group 1 (found by correlating the screenshot's blue pixels with renders of all jungle islands with rivers; the view is the 2494 × 1355 px window at scene pixel (-96, 1934), and the rebuild's render of that window matches the screenshot pixel for pixel in position, mean difference 2.7 of 255 per channel from the water animation).

**Measure** (`tools/river_qa.py`, `src/tools/river_qa/main.cpp`): the QA tool runs the viewer's own chunked calls (`Scene::ground_with_probe`, `Scene::water_field_from`), so it measures the field that the CPU path and the GPU compositor both draw; the GPU only shades it differently (checked below). Per scene and zoom group: O = pixels where the original draws river water (the river and waterfall/mouth sprites with the original's depth test; the sprites are a 50 % checkerboard, so O is closed over 3 × 3 pixels and stray single pixels at a bed's rim are ignored), W = pixels where enhanced water is drawn (field cover set and depth ≥ 0, i.e. alpha ≥ 0.5). **Gap** = O pixel with no W within `th/10 + 1` px (5 / 3 / 2 px at groups 0 / 1 / 2; closer misses are the two soft edges differing by a pixel). Connectivity: 8-connected components of O, and of W near O; an O component is **split** when it touches more than one water component and **lost** when it touches none.

**Root causes** (before the fix, 72 islands with rivers in the 5 climates that have them, 13 scenarios; Polar has no rivers):
1. *Wrong bed surface (dominant).* The enhanced river test compared the river level with `sea::smooth_height`, the cubic **B-spline** through the corner heights that the sea's waterline uses (right there: an approximating spline hides the tile steps). A B-spline does not pass through its control points. A river piece's height byte is its lowest corner (`D_256x256_0045_5B`: 152 of 184 pieces have z = the minimum of their 4 × 4 corners; 181 of 184 have a corner 16 above z, so the banks stand 16 units over the bed), the river surface is z + 9 (`kRiverLevel`), and the weights of the spline at a corner are 1/6, 2/3, 1/6 per axis, so the floor of a channel one corner wide is lifted by 16/3 = 5.3 units in a straight channel and by (1 − (2/3)²) · 16 = 8.9 units at a bend or dead end: the river depth (9), so no water depth was left at the thalweg and the soft edge (±0.35 units) cut it. Of the 6,418 gap pixels of this island at group 2 none lay where no river level existed (`IslandWater::river_level` nullopt) and none under sea water: every one had a level and a bed above it, 0–2 units above for 3,169, 2–4 for 2,968, 4–8 for 251, more than 8 for 20, and 10 with the level above the bed but outside the river branch's soft edge.
2. *Different coverage rules.* The original draws a river sprite pixel where the terrain seen there is at most the piece's own height byte + 10 units (`kRiverTolerance`, the triangulated terrain); enhanced compared a level averaged over overlapping pieces and blurred twice (piece heights differ by several units along a river) + 9 with the B-spline plus ±3 units of fixed noise, so even with a correct bed the two edges could not agree.
3. No cause on the GPU side: the compositor reads the same field; the rendered images of the owner's window have the same gap share on the CPU and the GPU (below).

**Fix** (`sea::river_bed_height`, `Scene::apply_fall_masks`; CPU and GPU alike because both read the field):
- The river bed is an interpolating **Catmull-Rom** surface through the corner heights (clamped to the four corners of the cell, so it never overshoots) plus half the shore noise (`kRiverBankNoise`), instead of the waterline spline. Banks stay organic (noise, soft edge) and the bed keeps its depth.
- The painted bed is a floor: the river pieces' own sprites, drawn with the original's depth test into a mask (`IslandData::river_sprites`), are closed over the checkerboard, blurred like the falls' shapes (≈ 4 px at group 0) and give the water depth `max(physical depth, (alpha − 0.5) · 4 units)` (`kBedEdge`, `kBedDepth`). So enhanced water never ends short of the original's water, even where a bed is only a few pixels wide where two pieces meet (these necks were 1–3 px hairlines with alpha 0.5 before); where the physical bed is deeper, the physical depth stays. Soft edges stay (the blur), original water is unchanged (sprites are drawn as before), nothing touches sim state.

**Result** (`river_qa`, same metric before and after; "before" = the code of the previous commit):

## Water against objects (enhanced water, GPU; not in the original)
Owner feedback 2026-10-18 (on the river-mouth images): rocks sit on top of the water like the ships did, the water seemingly floating under them; the water should interact with the environment in general. Objects are baked into the chunk objects layer, so the effect works per pixel of that layer:
- **Base plane** (`Canvas::base`, `frame::Chunk::base`, chunks built for the GPU only): while the objects are blitted, each object pixel records how many rows it lies above that object's lowest pixel in its column; front objects overwrite as their colours do. Columns whose lowest pixel lies more than 0.6 tile heights above the frame anchor (the ground point) do not touch the ground (foliage, rigging) and get 255.
- **Shader** (`object_in_water`, `objects_fs` in `gpu.wgsl`): the water field at the object's lowest pixel in the column gives the water depth there; the water level in pixels is that depth (at most `OBJ_MAX_DEPTH` = 6 HEIGHT units) × lift. Object pixels below it are tinted towards the water and faded into the sea or river drawn underneath, with a small ripple; at the sea a broken foam line marks the contact, **not on rivers** (owner: foam only at the sea).
- Switch: `GpuLimits::object_waterline` (frame uniform `objects_water`). Original water and the CPU path are unchanged; test: `gpu_test.cpp` "enhanced water meets objects standing in it" (a river mouth of `Single_01` with rocks: 1,928 pixels differ with enhanced water, none with original water).
The GPU shader now differs from the Rust reference's: `crates/render/src/gpu.wgsl` is a frozen copy for `gpu.rs` (the Rust code gets no new features).

## Ships in the water (enhanced water, GPU; not in the original)
Owner request 2026-10-18 (viewer test): the ships' hulls end in a hard edge and look like pictures lying on the water. The original frames show the whole hull down to the keel (`SCHIFF` figures; e.g. the 258 × 255 frame at group 0 has its anchor, the point on the water surface, at row 217 and the hull's lowest pixels at rows 207–254), and the original draws them on the sea unchanged; with the original water the rebuild keeps that, pixel for pixel.

With enhanced water the GPU compositor draws ships itself (after all island objects, as `Scene::draw_units`; chunks of a `FrameRenderer(…, false)` leave them out) and `ship_in_water` in `gpu.wgsl` treats the lowest band of the hull as under water:
- **Keel line:** per pixel column the lowest opaque pixel of the frame; rows more than `SHIP_BAND` (40 px at group 0) above the anchor never count, so rigging, bowsprit and anchor ropes are not taken for the hull.
- **Waterline:** `SHIP_DRAFT` (6 px) above the keel line, moved by the sea: with the wave simulation the surface elevation at the ship (`fluid_sample`, HEIGHT units × lift) raises and lowers it and the slope between bow and stern (a third of the frame width either side) pitches it; without the simulation a slow analytic swell; plus small ripples along the hull.
- **Below the waterline:** the hull is tinted towards the water underneath (`under_tex`) and faded into the sea (fade length `SHIP_FADE`, 3 px), with light ripple streaks near the surface; a little broken foam continues up to `SHIP_SKIRT` (3 px) below the keel line, so the sunken hull has no hard lower edge.
- **Foam:** a broken line along the waterline (noise drifting with time).
All lengths are pixels at zoom group 0 scaled by the tile height; they are tuning, not evidence. Switch: `GpuLimits::ship_waterline` (frame uniform `ship_water`). The CPU path does not draw it (CPU enhanced water was already a simpler fallback). Tests: `gpu_test.cpp` "ships drawn by the compositor match the CPU path" (original water, all zoom groups, within 1 per channel) and "enhanced water: ships sit in the water, only at the hull bottom" (pixels change only inside the ship's frame below the band's top, never with original water).

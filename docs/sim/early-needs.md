7. **Arithmetic (hand-derived) versus elapsed time.** *Arithmetic only:* one trip credits 768 raw grass (3 t); the sheep farm turns 768 into **52 wool in 9 steps** (8 x 87 -> 6 each, then 72 raw -> 4), leaving 1 raw; 52 wool make 28 cloth in 5 weaver steps. A weaver's carrier takes goods only from a source holding >= 1 t (256) of them. *Elapsed time is a separate question and these numbers say nothing about sustainable supply.* A lower bound for the **first pickup of wool** (assumptions: the first eligible farm step is at tick 500 and every later 500-tick step sends one more animal, one figure per eligible step as vector F-fresh/`send`; grass always within reach; zero walking time; raw limits nothing after the first trips): raw grass reaches the farm only when a trip ends (step C). The first trip is sent at tick 500, its figure's first visit completes 5 elapsed ticks, and it waits 3 cuts x 6,500 = 19,500 ticks before it can walk home, so the first 768 raw arrive no earlier than about **500 + 5 + 19,500 = 20,005 ticks**. Productive farm steps therefore begin at the next 500-tick boundary, about **20,500**, and 43 productive steps (43 x 6 = 258 >= 256; 43 x 87 = 3,741 raw = about 5 trips, whose animals were sent at 500, 1,000, ... and return from about 20,005 on) end no earlier than 20,500 + 42 x 500 = **about 41,500 ticks** for the first 256 wool at the farm. That is a lower bound, not an expectation; the carrier trip (weaver input 11 per 500-tick step, 24 steps for 256 wool = 12,000 ticks of weaver steps), the cloth pickup and the household purchases come on top, and real walking too: all **measured at N3**. (An earlier version of this section cited about 21,500 ticks; that assumed all five animals attached at the first step and counted farm steps before any raw could arrive: wrong.)
